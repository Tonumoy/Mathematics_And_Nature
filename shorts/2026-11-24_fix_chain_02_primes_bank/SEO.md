## Description
Every time you open your bank app, two secret numbers guard your money. 🔐
2,000 years of locks in one chain — and every fix created a new problem:
❗ Send a secret → ✅ Caesar shifts each letter by 3 (A→D)
❗ Count the letters (al-Kindi, 800s) → cracked
✅ Long secret keys → ❗ but someone has to deliver the key
✅ 1977: a padlock anyone can snap shut, only you can open (RSA — two big prime numbers: 61 × 53 = 3233 is instant, going backwards is brutally hard)
Today's lock number (RSA-2048) is 617 digits long. Nobody has ever cracked one.
❗ A Google researcher (Gidney, 2025) estimated a big enough quantum computer could crack it in under a week. That machine doesn't exist yet.
✅ 2024: new US standard locks (NIST ML-KEM, built on lattice puzzles) with no known quantum shortcut.
Will your bank change its locks in time? 👇

Sources: Suetonius (Caesar); al-Kindi; Rivest–Shamir–Adleman 1977; RSA Factoring Challenge (RSA-2048); Gidney arXiv:2505.15917 (2025); NIST FIPS 203 (Aug 2024). The 617-digit number on screen is an illustration.
