Sound drew this. 🔊 Sand on a steel plate, one note at a time. Watch where the sand goes, and wait for the highest note.

Why the patterns? At a note the plate "likes" (a resonance), it splits into patches. Neighbouring patches move opposite ways, one up while the other goes down. Where they meet, the plate stays perfectly still (nodal lines). Sand gets kicked off the shaking parts and piles up on those still lines. Higher note → more patches → more lines.

What you see: a 24 cm × 24 cm steel plate, 1 mm thick, driven at its centre. Every pixel is our own render. The shapes are not drawn by hand: our code solved the real bending-plate equation for this exact plate, then let 80,000 grains of sand hop wherever it shakes.

The maths: Kirchhoff plate equation  D∇⁴w = ρh·ω²·w,  with D = Eh³ / 12(1−ν²)
Solved with the Ritz method (729 Legendre functions, free edges). Check: our first frequencies match the textbook values for a free square plate (13.47, 19.60, 24.27, 34.80).
Ten of this plate's centre-driven resonances: 102, 269, 517, 711, 1,186, 1,455, 1,973, 2,529, 3,058, 3,950 Hz.
The twist: frequency grows with thickness (ω ∝ h). Twice as thick = double the pitch for the same shape.

Which one would you hang on your wall: the 102 Hz ring or the 3,950 Hz lace? 👇
