## Description
Rock that floats on the sea. This is a real photo from space: NASA's Landsat 8 saw a raft of floating pumice near Tonga on 13 Aug 2019.

Why it floats: pumice is lava that was full of volcanic gas. The bubbles froze in, so the stone is mostly air.
ρ_stone = (1 − φ) · ρ_rock  →  with ρ_rock ≈ 2.5 g/cm³ and φ ≈ 0.72 air, ρ ≈ 0.70 g/cm³ < sea water 1.025 → it floats.
Below φ ≈ 0.59 it sinks. Water slowly gets into the bubbles, so pumice floats for days to years (bigger floats longer), then sinks (Fauria et al., Earth and Planetary Science Letters, 2017).

In the Ramayana, Rama's army must cross the sea to Lanka to rescue Sita, and Nala builds the bridge (setu). Today a chain of shoals between Dhanushkodi (India) and Mannar (Sri Lanka), called Ram Setu / Adam's Bridge, is still seen from space. ISRO's 2024 ICESat-2 map (Scientific Reports) traced it for ~29 km and found 99.98% of it under the sea; old records say a storm in 1480 broke it (Britannica). Its origin is still debated; the shoals are sand with coral, not pumice. Happy Diwali 🪔
