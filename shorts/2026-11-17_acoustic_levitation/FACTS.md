# SC#12 FACTS (checked 17 Nov 2026)
- Single-axis acoustic levitator "TinyLev": 72 ultrasonic transducers (two caps of 36), 40 kHz, levitates water drops, polystyrene beads, small insects. Marzo, Barnes & Drinkwater, Rev. Sci. Instrum. 88, 085105 (2017). https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk / pubmed 28863691
- 40 kHz is above human hearing (~20 kHz) -> "too high to hear".
- Opposing waves form a standing wave; pressure nodes every half wavelength: λ/2 = c/(2f) = 343/(2*40,000) m = 4.29 mm (c = 343 m/s air, 20 °C).
- Small dense objects (drops) are pushed to (just below, gravity) the pressure nodes by the acoustic radiation force (Gor'kov potential). Several nodes can each hold a drop.
- Too little sound pressure: radiation force < weight, drop falls. Too much: drop flattens to an oblate disc, then becomes unstable and atomises/bursts (Lee, Anilkumar & Wang, J. Fluid Mech. 226, 25 (1991); Danilov & Mironov, JASA 1992).
- Drug use: Argonne National Lab (2012) levitated drug solution drops so they dry without touching a container, giving amorphous (more soluble) drug forms. ScienceDaily 13 Sep 2012 https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/09/120913123522.htm
- On-screen sound level readout (150-174 dB) = illustrative scale for the dial; real levitators run roughly 150-165 dB at the antinodes. Kept as a dial label, not a claim in the VO.
- Fall shown in slow motion (x18), labelled on screen.
