Saturn's rings are wider than twenty Earths, and about ten metres thick.
Shrink them to a football field: thinner than paper.
What flattened them? Gravity alone? Nope.
Look closer: billions of ice chunks.
When two bump, they lose up-and-down speed,
but keep their spin.
Bump by bump, the cloud flattens into a sheet.
Now make the ice perfectly bouncy.
It stays puffy. Real ice? Flat again.
Galaxies flatten the same way. Nature is running on code.
