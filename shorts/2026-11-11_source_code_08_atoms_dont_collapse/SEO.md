TITLE: Why Don't Atoms Collapse? ⚛️ The real equation says no #shorts

DESCRIPTION:
Why don't atoms collapse? An electron circling a nucleus should leak light and spiral in within about 0.000 000 000 016 s. It doesn't.
What you see: our own render. First the "planet" atom crashing, then the real radial Schrödinger equation (finite-difference solver, 1,500 grid points, imaginary-time relaxation) letting a squeezed electron cloud find its size. Each of the 20,000 dots is a place the electron could be found, drawn from the solver's own probability.
Squeeze the cloud and its jiggle (kinetic energy) climbs like ħ²/2ma². The pull of the nucleus only grows like ke²/a. Total E(a) = ħ²/2ma² − ke²/a has a floor at a₀ = ħ²/(m k e²) = 0.0529 nm, energy −13.6 eV (our solver: −13.604 eV).
Twist: turn Planck's constant ħ down and a₀ shrinks like ħ²: the atom collapses. With the real ħ it snaps back.
Sources: CODATA 2018 Bohr radius 5.29177×10⁻¹¹ m (NIST); NIST hydrogen ionization energy 13.598 eV; Griffiths, Introduction to Quantum Mechanics (hydrogen atom; classical collapse ~1.6×10⁻¹¹ s); Feynman Lectures Vol. III, 2-4 (size of an atom).
