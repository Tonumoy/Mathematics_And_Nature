TITLE: What Happens If You Fall Into a Black Hole? #shorts

DESCRIPTION:
What would a black hole do to your body? Not crush you: it stretches you. Your feet are closer to the hole than your head, so gravity pulls them harder, and the difference pulls you apart.

What you see
• Our own ray-traced black hole (real light paths around a Schwarzschild black hole) and a 3D astronaut built from signed-distance shapes, relit by the disk, all rendered in code
• X-ray: the real solver. Every piece of the body falls on its own path (radial geodesic, d²r/dτ² = −GM/r²); the body is a spring chain that snaps when stretched too far (body strength is a simulation choice)
• One dial: the black hole's mass. Bigger hole = gentler edge

The code
• Stretch across your body: Δa = 2GM·L / r³ (radial tidal acceleration; M = hole mass, r = distance, L = your height)
• 10-sun black hole: edge (event horizon) 29.5 km from the centre; stretch on a 1.8 m person at the edge ≈ 1.9 × 10⁸ m/s² ≈ 19 million g; even 10× further out it's ~19,000 g → torn apart far outside the edge
• Sagittarius A* (centre of our galaxy, ~4 million Suns): edge ≈ 12 million km out; stretch at the edge ≈ 0.001 m/s² ≈ 0.0001 g → you'd cross it and feel nothing (then you couldn't get out)
• Fall from 100× the edge of the 10-sun hole: 0.15 s of your own time, shown slowed down
